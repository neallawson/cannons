Help me develop a plan and then the code for a browser-game.
Here are some of the central features:
- The game can be played in single player (AI) mode or, ideally, multi-player.
- It's always one player against another in a turn-based strategy game.
- The game screen shows a castle on a hill on the left and a castle on a hill on the right with a valley in between. One has a red flag, one a blue flag.
- Each castle has a cannon, and that is the sole weapon of engagement.
- A player shoots the cannon toward the enemy castle by moving the barrel up and down with the mouse and entering a numerical value (within some bounds) for the cannon's "charge", or power. The cannon is fired, and the projectile flies according to the angle and charge value. If it impacts the castle, a "chunk" is taken out of the castle at that location, ideally taking into account the velocity and angle at impact to accuractly determine the "chunk" that's removed. The first player to either disable the other player's cannon or to hit the magazine room in the lower level of the castle (after knocking away enough chunks to reach it), thus blowing up the castle.
- For multiplayer, the game uses peer-to-peer.
- The game needs a peer-to-peer connection system for player's to enter an IP and "ping" another player for a game connection. IP's and associated names can be saved in a local file if desired.
- Use 2d graphics for the views of the player's castles and the valley in between.
- The angle and power of the cannon should be sensitive, and the flags at both locations should fly in a manner that indicates the wind speed.
- The wind speed can vary between the two castles and should be taken into account for the projectile's flight, thus making the shots less repeatable and slightly more unpredictable.

Yes, I ultimately want a weather system as you described. The wind will be the fundamental variable from that system that affects gameplay, but the visual effects and smooth weather events will add a level of realism to the game that is hard to match with any other technique.
One last thing: I'd like a flag that visually shows the wind direction and approximate strength. We can print the actual wind speed value beside the flag for specificity to the user. So, in summary:
1. a generic Perlin noise module for wind and weather effects.
2. a wind module for continuous wind fluctuation in a realistic manner.
3. a weather module as already described.
4. a flag to serve as a visual indicator for the wind direction and approximate strength. Probably should put this in the UI.
